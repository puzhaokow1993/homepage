Speaker. (Cynthia) Oh, Hui Joo
Title. Riemann-Liouville and Caputo type fractional-order derivatives
Abstract. Fractional calculus provides an essential mathematical framework for modeling anomalous diffusion, memory effects, and complex dynamical systems. This presentation provides an introduction to the foundations of fractional derivatives, centering on the fundamental tension between the Riemann-Liouville and Caputo type fractional-order derivatives. In addition, we discuss some key algebraic and differential lemmas essential for fractional-order analysis, alongside the one- and two-parameter Mittag-Leffler functions.

Speaker. Su, Wu-Xuan
Title. Topological variant spread model
Abstract. In this talk, we introduce the notion of a topological variant spread model, in which each type evolves through a finite sequence of intermediate states before initiating the prescribed spreading mechanism, thereby providing a unified framework for modeling spreading phenomena involving incubation periods and mutation processes. We derive explicit formulas for spread rates in terms of the positive eigenvectors of the associated \(\xi\)-matrices, establish spectral characterizations of spread speeds, and investigate how intermediate-state dynamics affect long-term growth. In particular, we prove that increasing the invariant time strictly decreases the spread speed under suitable assumptions, while, in the general variant setting, the spread speed does not necessarily decrease over time; instead, it is determined by the species produced by the final mutation.

Speaker. Huang, Bo-Chao
Title. An Introduction to Infinite Countable Matrices
Abstract. This talk mainly serves as a basic introduction to infinite countable matrices, extending classical linear algebra from finite dimensions to countable state spaces. We first establish the basic definitions and related properties of eigenvalues and eigenvectors for infinite-dimensional matrices. In addition, with certain settings under the countable framework, we extend the famous Perron-Frobenius Theorem. Furthermore, we will introduce a finite approximation framework, demonstrating how to approximate the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of countable matrices through sequences of finite submatrices.

Speaker. Huang, Bo-Chao
Title. Spread Rates of 1-Spread Models with Countably Infinite Types
Abstract. This talk investigates the spread rates of 1-spread models defined on countably infinite types, extending traditional topological models originating from finite symbol sets to countably infinite models. We examine the propagation dynamics governed by countably infinite \(\xi\)-matrices, where the \(\alpha\)-scrambling property plays a crucial role in preventing dynamic divergence and ensuring strong ergodicity. By overcoming the problem of interchanging limits and infinite sums in the countable setting, we establish a rigorous analytical framework and formulas for spread rates.
